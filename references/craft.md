# The positive half

Everything in `tells.md` is a removal. Removals alone give you a draft with things taken out,
which is a different failure from the one you started with and often a worse read. A piece that
has been de-slopped and nothing else is cold, flat, and correct.

This file is what goes in. Reach for it after the edit pass, and reach for it hard when the
register is evocation or narrative.

```txt
Zinsser named the two qualities he was hunting through the whole of On Writing Well:
humanity and warmth. Neither survives an edit pass on its own.
```

---

## 1. Put an image where an abstraction is

The strongest single move in this file. Find the sentence carrying the most weight and ask
whether the reader can see, hear, or feel anything in it.

```txt
before   the profound emotional experience of listening to music
after    the deep stirring of the soul when we listen to Mozart's Requiem

before   early stone tools conferred a significant hunting advantage
after    these were primitive, but could tear through skin and muscle

before   we want the piece to have emotional impact
after    we want to make the hairs on the back of the neck stand up

before   humans are a small part of a vast universe
after    a speck of dust in the vastness of the universe
```

An image is not decoration and it is not the same as a citation. A date makes a claim
checkable. An image makes a reader present. Most drafts that have been through a de-slopping
pass are starved of the second and stuffed with the first.

## 2. Let the writer want something

Prose with no attitude reads as prose by nobody. The stance can be any of these, and the
choice belongs to the piece:

```txt
delight       the writer is thrilled and says so
unease        the writer is bothered and cannot fully say why
conviction    the writer thinks this is right and will defend it
grief         the writer has lost something
mischief      the writer is enjoying themselves
```

What is not available is neutrality with the affect sanded off. "There are arguments on both
sides" is the absence of a stance, and readers register absence as evasion.

**Warmth is compatible with rigour.** The BBC's *Being Human* essay is factually careful and
openly enthusiastic at the same time. Precision does not require coldness, and the habit of
mistaking flatness for seriousness is one of the strongest pulls in machine-written prose.

## 3. Use rhythm on purpose

Cadence is a tool. `tells.md` flags cadence that stands in for an argument; that is a different
thing from cadence that carries one.

**The cumulative sentence.** A long sentence that keeps adding, each clause opening the frame
wider, is one of the best devices in English and is not the same as an overloaded sentence. The
test is whether the subject stays in view.

> We will look deep into the mind at what drives our behaviour, meet extraordinary humans who
> have unlocked the secrets of a long and healthy life, take a trip through 2000 years of
> civilisation, journey into the human body on our path to adulthood, experience the drama of
> extraordinary human rituals that hope to cheat death, and watch what happens to our bodies in
> the hours, days, and months after we die.

Seventy-seven words, one subject, six clauses, and it accelerates. Do not break it.

**The rhythmic series.** Three concrete nouns in a row is a figure with a long history, and it
works when the members are things you can picture: *danger, hope, and love*; *the hours, days,
and months after we die*. The tell is the abstract triad reaching for completeness:
*correctness, maintainability, and scalability*. Judge the members, not the count.

**The short sentence after a long one.** Earn it, then use it.

## 4. Let a line land

A closing beat is allowed to be good. The rule against quotable lines exists to stop a piece
manufacturing profundity it has not paid for, and it is not a rule against memorable sentences.

```txt
earned      the piece has built the idea, and the line names what was built
             "From this point on the pace of change was electrifying."
             "we continue our ascent into insignificance"
unearned    the line is doing the work the missing argument should have done
             "What makes us human, in the end, is not what we are. It's what we
              choose to become."
```

The difference is whether the preceding paragraphs paid for it. Flatten the second. Keep the
first.

## 5. Address someone

"We" and "you" put a person in the room. An evocation in particular usually wants "we", because
it is inviting the reader into a shared experience rather than reporting one.

The transaction is between two people. A piece that hides both of them has thrown away most of
what it had.

## 6. Give the reader something to be glad about

Not forced uplift, and not a generic hopeful ending. Something the writer actually finds good,
stated plainly. Curiosity, luck, an unlikely fact, a thing that turned out better than expected.

A piece can end on wonder if the wonder is real and the piece earned it. The prohibition is on
manufactured optimism about the future, which is a different move.

## The affect check

Run this last, after the edit pass and before delivering:

```txt
Can the reader see anything? Name the images. If there are none, the piece is information.
Does the writer want something? If the stance is neutral, say why that is right for this piece.
Is there a sentence the reader might remember? Is it earned?
Did the edit pass make this colder than the draft was? If so, what did it take out, and
  should some of it go back?
```

That last question is the important one. Compare the rewrite against the draft for warmth, not
only for tells. If the draft was alive and the rewrite is correct, the rewrite is worse.
